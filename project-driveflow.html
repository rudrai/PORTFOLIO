DriveFlow CRM

A lead-management CRM designed for a fictional auto dealership — four core screens and three automations, mapped from problem to workflow.

Overview

DriveFlow is a product design case study built as a design-interview assignment: a lead-management CRM for a fictional automotive dealership. The brief called for four screens working as one system — lead listing, lead detail, a kanban pipeline, and a business-manager dashboard — plus automations that make the workflow actually move on its own.

The problem

Dealership sales teams generate leads faster than they can manually track them. Leads stall between stages, business managers lack a single view of pipeline health, and reps waste time on manual handoffs that a system should handle automatically.

The approach

I started from the sales workflow itself, not the screens: how does a lead enter the system, who touches it, and where does it get stuck. That produced the four-screen structure — a listing view to triage new leads, a detail view for the full record, a kanban board for pipeline visibility, and a dashboard for the business manager to see the whole funnel at once. From there I designed three automations that remove manual steps at the exact points where leads were stalling.

Key features

  • Lead listing screen for fast triage and filtering of incoming leads
  • Lead detail view consolidating contact history and deal status
  • Kanban board visualizing every lead's stage across the pipeline
  • Business-manager dashboard summarizing funnel health at a glance
  • Three end-to-end automations designed to remove manual handoffs

Outcome

The result is a complete product case study — problem framing, four connected screens, and automation logic documented as a real system would need it, not just static mockups. It's the project that best represents how I approach product management: starting from the workflow, not the interface.
